# VTS 1970-1976 Datsun 240Z/260Z/280Z Poly Tank Installation Guide

Source: Vintage Tank Solutions (VTS) — [`docs/vts-tank-install-1.jpeg`](../docs/vts-tank-install-1.jpeg), [`docs/vts-tank-install-2.jpeg`](../docs/vts-tank-install-2.jpeg)

> Our poly tanks are to be used only with compatible aftermarket fuel pump hangers and fuel senders designed for use with polyethylene tanks.

## Installation Steps

1. Remove the original equipment fuel tank per the Factory Service Manual (FSM) procedure. Remove the four (4) rubber insulator pads from the original tank if they are being reused on the poly tank. **We offer a custom cut adhesive backed rubber insulator set specifically for this tank on our website Products page.** Remove and set aside the two (2) original equipment steel fuel tank bands and hardware; these will also be reused if in acceptable condition. **NOTE: Installation of the poly tank only on 1970 through 1974 models (240Z and early 260Z) will require longer hardware if reusing the oem steel fuel tank bands.** Alternatively, the slightly longer 1974.5 to 1976 oem steel fuel tank bands can be used on the 1970-1974 models instead, or new 1974.5-1976 steel bands can be purchased from online Datsun Z vendors. **We offer a longer hardware set on our website Products page if reusing the original steel bands.**
2. Test fit the poly fuel tank under the hatch floor to confirm that no vehicle modifications/systems interfere with the placement of the tank, including: electrical components, mounting bands, fuel filler hose, fuel hoses, and exhaust components. **If any part of your exhaust system is less than 2.00 inches (51mm) from the poly tank, we recommend using an adhesive (sticky) reflective and insulated thermal barrier on that exhaust section to prevent the possibility of melting the poly tank.**
3. Plan out the routing of fuel and vent hoses from the poly tank to the soft or hard fuel lines, vapor tank, and/or venting system to be used on the vehicle. Additionally, plan out the routing of the fuel pump and sending unit wiring to the electrical system of the vehicle. **The poly tank MUST be vented to a factory vapor tank, aftermarket vapor/fuel overflow tank, rollover vent valve, and/or alternative venting system that is mounted higher than the maximum fuel fill level of the poly tank. NO EXCEPTIONS!**
4. Determine the best mounting location for the aftermarket internal fuel pump hanger assembly (for EFI) or bolt-in fuel pick-up assembly (for carburetors/external fuel pumps) on the large flat mounting pad of the passenger side (fuel filler neck side) so that the fuel pump inlet/filter bag positions in the deepest section of the tank. Make note of the poly tank internal baffle wall location in reference to the placement of the fuel pump assembly. Some brands utilize long metal trays or pick-up filters on their fuel pumps, **be certain this won't interfere with the baffle wall when determining in-tank pump placement. Maximum mounting depth is 10.48" (266.0mm).**
5. Determine the best mounting location for the aftermarket fuel gauge sending unit on the large flat mounting pad so that it doesn't interfere with the fuel pump hanger or internal fuel pump components. Maximum mounting depth is 10.48" (266.0mm).
6. Mark the locations on the top of the mounting pad for your components and drill/cut to the manufacturer's suggested diameter. Steel hole saws can be used for this and are commonly available in a variety of sizes.
7. After all holes are cut and/or drilled, remove any extra polyethylene flashing with a razor blade. Either carefully cut the excess away from the tank or scrape the blade at an angle over the corners of the cut/drilled holes to make flush and smooth. Then blow out, water rinse, or vacuum up any remaining material inside the tank prior to installing any fuel components.
8. Once the fuel pump hanger/fuel pick-up assembly and/or sending unit are mounted securely to the tank, install the four (4) original OR aftermarket rubber insulators (as noted in Step 1) within the raised outlined sections of the tank. **This is REQUIRED, the poly tank CANNOT be in direct contact with any metal surface or material which can potentially compromise the integrity of the tank.** Depending on the head height of the aftermarket fuel pump hanger/fuel pick-up assembly and sending unit installed, you may opt to use thin or thick rubber insulators. We recommend bonding a weather-proof insulator, such as neoprene rubber, with a minimum thickness of ¼ inch thick to the poly tank. Stacking and bonding more than one insulator on top of another may be required to achieve a level tank installation. *Installation Note: If very thick insulators are used, the tank neck may no longer align with the OEM fuel filler tube inlet; therefore, an aftermarket fuel filler tube may be required.* **New adhesive backed rubber insulators designed specifically for this poly tank can be purchased on our website Products page.**
9. Before final installation, inspect and/or replace the steel fuel tank bands; it's common to find they have rusted and corroded which can compromise the integrity of securing the fuel tank to the vehicle with the added weight of fuel. Also inspect and/or replace the rubber straps as they must be used between the bands and the poly tank. New rubber straps can be bonded to the metal bands; we recommend a weather-proof rubber, such as neoprene, with a thickness of at least 1/16 inch (1.59mm). **New adhesive backed rubber fuel tank straps can be purchased on our website Products page.**
10. Raise, support, and install the poly fuel tank, locating it to the factory tank position. Secure the two fuel tank bands around the tank with the hardware and loosely hang the tank. Ensure you have all the fuel hoses and electrical wiring properly labeled prior to securing the poly tank to the hatch floor. Connect the original equipment rubber fuel tank filler tube to the inlet neck of the tank. Heating the rubber tube end with a heat gun may be required slip the tube over the poly tank neck. Use a properly sized/designed steel clamp on the filler neck, <u>do not overtighten</u>.
11. Tighten the hardware and align the steel bands within the poly tank strap recess areas while confirming the poly tank is correctly positioned under the hatch floor. **Do NOT overtighten the tank bands.** For first time use/installation, the fuel tank bands only need to be snug. Ensure the fuel tank is secure and doesn't shift by attempting to move it in all directions by hand. **After fuel has been in the tank for at least 24 hours, inspect the bands again for a snug fit to ensure the tank doesn't shift around. Tighten the hardware only until the tank is secure.** Over-tightening the tank bands can result in damaging the poly tank.
12. The first time fuel is introduced into the poly tank, we recommend filling the tank to 85%-90% capacity. Fuel needs room to expand, avoid topping off the fuel tank and/or overfilling it. Check to ensure that no leaks are present. Prime your fuel pump to allow trapped air/vapor to purge, while testing the fuel system, and ensuring the vent/vapor tank is properly operating. If a leak is present, use your electric fuel pump or an alternative method (such as pulling fuel from the filler tube) to remove enough fuel from the tank to inspect and repair the leak.

## Fuel Pump Hanger and Fuel Sending Unit Installation Tips

Aftermarket fuel pump hangers, fuel pick-up assemblies, and fuel sending units come in a variety of sizes and designs. We have incorporated a large flat mounting pad on the highest section of the tank to accommodate the most popular options.

- It's recommended that the fuel pump pick-up filter is located in the deepest section of the tank. Determine where this location is best suited for your fuel pump hanger design and pump hanger assembly depth. Examine the cross-section shape of the tank with the baffle wall and reference it to the top of the tank to determine the pump hanger base mounting location. The pump hanger base can only be secured to the flattest area on the top of the tank to ensure a leak-free seal. We recommend use of a hole saw on a drill to achieve the desired fuel pump/sender hole cut size. **Measure multiple times and triple check your options before drilling or cutting any holes in the poly tank. Repairs CANNOT be made to the poly tank once it's been drilled or cut.** If you make an error cutting or drilling into the poly tank, there are several options to remedy the issue. Please contact us for suggestions.
- Refer to the manufacturer's installation instructions for your aftermarket fuel pump hanger/fuel pick-up assembly, paying close attention to the gaskets used with poly fuel tanks to ensure a leak free seal. An internal steel C-ring may be required to secure the fuel pump base onto the poly tank depending on the product or brand selected. **NOTE: Our poly tanks are NOT equipped with a vent and the tank MUST be vented to operate properly.** ***CAUTION:*** If the fuel tank is not vented a rupture can occur from excessive pressures. Ensure that you install a non-kinked vent hose to one of the many options for vapor/fuel collection listed in Step 3.
- Refer to the aftermarket sending unit manufacturer's instructions to ensure correct installation for a leak free seal. An internal steel C-ring may be required to secure the sending unit base onto the poly tank depending on the product or brand selected.

## Specifications

- Made from cross-linked polyethylene
- Lightweight one-piece design (13-pounds VS oem at 18-22 pounds)
- Slash cut corners to aid in exhaust pipe clearance
- UV, chemical, and corrosion resistant
- Integrated rear diffuser

## Fuel Component Size Limitations

The flat upper fuel component mounting pad dimensions are:

- Length 9.00" (229mm)
- Width 7.40" (188mm)
- Max depth/height 10.48" (266mm) for installation of fuel pumps/senders

Per the dimensions above, we recommend selecting a fuel sender with a length of 10.00 inches. **We offer a custom fuel sender for use with the factory Datsun fuel gauge or any programmable ohms operated fuel gauge on our website Products page.**
